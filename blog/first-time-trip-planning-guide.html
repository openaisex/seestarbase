First Time at Starbase? A Practical Trip-Planning Guide

September 2026

Most first-time visitors trip up on the same few basics: where to stay, how to get around, and whether a rental car is really necessary. This guide sticks to the practical stuff, so you can skip the guesswork.

1. Where to stay — South Padre Island vs. Brownsville

This is the biggest decision you'll make.

South Padre Island — The beach is right there, and it's one of the most convenient places to watch a launch (Isla Blanca Park especially). More restaurants and things to do, more of a vacation feel. Downside: usually pricier than Brownsville, and hotels can sell out or spike in price around a launch window. It's also a detour to reach Starbase itself — about 45–60 minutes one way. Good fit if you want to combine the trip with some beach time, you're traveling with family, or you expect to wait out a delayed launch window.

Brownsville — Closer to BRO airport, more hotel options and generally cheaper, and a more direct route to Highway 4 (about 25–40 minutes). Downside: no beach atmosphere, and if the road closes on launch day, viewing isn't as convenient as it is from the island. Good fit if you're on a budget, mainly here for the launch itself, and only staying a night or two.

Rough rule of thumb: launch-focused trip with a few extra days → South Padre Island. Tight budget, short stay → Brownsville is the better value. Want to be as close to the site as possible → a private campground like Rocket Ranch is an option, but book ahead.

2. Do you really need a rental car?

Almost certainly, yes. There's no reliable public transit to Starbase or Boca Chica Beach. Getting from the airport to South Padre Island, and then out to viewing spots, depends on having your own vehicle.

  • Renting from Brownsville/South Padre Island International Airport (BRO) puts you closest to Starbase.
  • Harlingen (HRL) is a workable alternative, but adds extra driving.
  • Rental demand rises around launch windows — book ahead if you can.
  • Fill the tank yourself before returning the car; airport return fuel prices are usually steep.

3. Phone signal and navigation pitfalls

  • Signal gets weak or drops out along the back half of Highway 4 and at Boca Chica Beach, depending on your carrier.
  • Download offline maps before you go (both Google Maps and Apple Maps support this).
  • Navigation apps sometimes route you toward a road that's actually closed. If you hit a barricade or checkpoint, don't push through — turn around as directed.
  • For real-time road status, text "BEACH" to 866-513-3475, or follow official City of Starbase notices.

4. Gas stations

Gas stations are plentiful in Brownsville and along the route to South Padre Island. Fill up before you turn onto Highway 4 toward Starbase — there's essentially nothing on that stretch, and the round trip to the end of the road and back covers over 20 miles of empty highway. Fuel on South Padre Island itself tends to run a bit more expensive than in Brownsville.

6. A few other things worth knowing

  • Passport: Worth carrying even if you're not planning to cross into Mexico — this is a border area, and checks happen occasionally.
  • Weather: Summers are hot and humid — sunscreen and water are essential. Hurricane season runs roughly June through November; keep an eye on forecasts if you're visiting then.
  • Launch schedules slip — often. Book lodging that allows free cancellation or date changes where possible, so a delay doesn't cost you money.
  • Cash: Some parking lots and small shops are cash-only. Keep a little on hand.

For a first trip, the combination that works best for most people is: stay on South Padre Island, rent a car, download offline maps ahead of time, and keep an eye on road-closure updates before you head out.
