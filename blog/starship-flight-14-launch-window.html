Starship Flight 14 now targeting NET September 28

Updated September 18, 2026

Update, September 18: SpaceX announced directly (via its official X account and spacex.com) that Flight 14 has slipped roughly six days, from NET September 22 to no earlier than Monday, September 28, 2026. The 75-minute window opens at 7:15 a.m. CDT (12:15 UTC), pending regulatory approval — the standard caveat SpaceX attaches to every launch date until liftoff.

SpaceX has not yet published a revised backup date or an updated FAA airspace advisory alongside this new target, so we don't have an exact window-close time or backup date to report yet. We'll update this page as soon as those are published — we won't guess at them in the meantime.

What we know so far

This would be Starship's first attempt at a full orbital flight, and the first flight to deploy Starlink V3 satellites into orbit. SpaceX's mission page lists 26 Starlink V3 satellites as the payload.

  • Primary date: NET September 28, 2026
  • Window opens: 7:15 a.m. CDT (12:15 UTC), ~75 minutes
  • Backup date: not yet published
  • Vehicles: Booster 21 + Ship 41 from Pad 2
  • Payload: 26 Starlink V3 satellites

Road and beach status

Road and beach closures for Flight 14 have not been formally announced by the City of Starbase as of this update. Closures are typically published 2–5 days before a launch attempt. Check the official City of Starbase notices and text "BEACH" to 866-513-3475 for real-time alerts before you travel.

Sources: SpaceX official X account and spacex.com Flight 14 mission page (schedule update, September 17–18, 2026). Previous target of September 22–23 was based on an earlier FAA airspace advisory and NASASpaceflight reporting, now superseded by SpaceX's own announcement.
